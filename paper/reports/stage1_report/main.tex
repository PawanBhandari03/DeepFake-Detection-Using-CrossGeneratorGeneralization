\chapter*{Abstract}
\onehalfspacing
Realistic AI-generated images, face-swapped videos and edited documents are easy to produce today, yet the person who receives such a file needs one answer that can be understood and checked. This project builds a web-based system that accepts an image, a video or a document, passes it to a specialised analyser and turns the evidence into a calibrated Trust Score from 0 to 100, a verdict (Likely Authentic, Needs Human Review or Likely AI-generated / Manipulated), a heatmap and a plain-language explanation. The work is carried out in three phases: image, video and document.

This preliminary report presents the requirements, the design and the first completed phase. The image analyser cuts ten $224\times224$ patches at native resolution, encodes them with a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 model and scores them with a small logistic head trained on degraded copies of real and AI-generated images. On 20 generators that were not seen in training it reaches an AUC of 0.982, against 0.831 for an EfficientNet-B0 baseline, and it accepts 92\% of real photographs. A variant that never saw an OpenAI or Google image still catches 84\% of ChatGPT images. Tests on photographs taken by the team and their ChatGPT and Gemini edits, a check for the visible Gemini watermark, and notices for drawings, screenshots and compressed files complete the first phase. The video and document analysers are designed and are planned for the next phases.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} deepfake detection, AI-generated images, cross-generator generalization, CLIP, calibration, explainable verification.
